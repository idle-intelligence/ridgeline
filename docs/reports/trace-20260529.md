Trace: `Trace-20260529T012521.json` (54 MB). Player saw hundreds of

## Verdict
- Mean frame **86 ms (~11 fps)**; **70% of frames > 50 ms** (the violation threshold); p95 250 ms.
- **`eng.step()` (Rust/WASM geometry generation) = 83–98% of every frame.** GPU 3.8 ms (never
  bound), `bufferSubData` ~1 ms, HUD textContent 0.5 ms, GC 0.1 ms, layout 0.05 ms — all negligible.
- Frame time **grows 40 ms → 200 ms over ~40 s** of flight (5×). JS heap flat (no leak).

## Why the altitude budget (178587c) didn't help here
The budget coarsens only above 1500 wu (boost 2 then 4). This session stayed at **low altitude**
(boost = 1, uncapped). Near-surface **lateral/forward movement progressively exposes more terrain
strips** in the visible cone → `wasm_step` balloons with no bound. fn[24] (ring/sub-ring emission)
has the steepest growth (6.4×).

## Conclusion → WebGPU
A hard vertex cap at low altitude would stop the violations but **sacrifice the near-surface detail**
the player wants. The structural fix is to move geometry generation OFF the CPU: a **WebGPU compute
pass** (the GO-verdict prototype) makes `eng.step()` ≈ 0 → rich detail at all altitudes AND 60 fps.
Decision: **productionize WebGPU, default when `navigator.gpu` available, WebGL2 fallback.**

(GPU peak rose to 11 ms/frame at high vertex counts — still not the bottleneck, but the WebGPU path
should keep emitted geometry sane via the same budget discipline.)
